# Elena

### *Tragedia lirica in tre atti*

## Libretto

Italian text with English translation. Written after the two governing melodies (the wedding chorus and Vittorio's vision-phrase) were composed, as the treatment requires, so that the prosody of every sung line sits under its music.

---

## A note on the verse

Closed numbers (arias, duets, choruses, the visions) use the regular metres of the Verdi-era libretto: *settenari*, *ottonari* and *endecasillabi*, rhymed, in the register of Piave, Cammarano and Boito. Scene-music and recitative use *versi liberi* in the manner of Illica and of the hermetic poets of the period (precise objects, light, the sea, the unnamed loss), so the dialogue can breathe at the speed of speech.

Three verbal threads are deliberately woven through the whole text:

| Thread | Where it is planted | Where it returns |
|---|---|---|
| **"sì"** (yes) | Elena's hope aria; her vow; the wedding refrain "*il vostro sì*" | Marco's letter ("*Questa sì*"); the final aria ("*dal giorno del mio sì*", "*quel sì lo ridirei*") |
| **"per sempre"** (forever) | The wedding refrain "*sia per sempre*" (let it be forever) | Vittorio's claim "*sei mia per sempre*" (you are mine forever), set to the same interval shape |
| **"nido"** (nest) | Elena's secret in Act I ("*lo tengo come un nido*") | Davide's understanding on the threshold ("*senti lui, nel nostro nido*") |

### Refinement of Vittorio's vision-texts

The treatment asks for four *ottonari* per appearance, with matching stresses, set to one fixed melody. The drafts were structurally right but several lines did not scan as *ottonari* (for example "*Nel tuo cuore io rimango*" counts seven syllables; "*Ovunque vai, io ti abito*" lands its stresses on syllables 2, 4 and 6; "*tutto è finito*" puts the accent on syllable 4 where the melody's strong beat falls on 5). The refined texts keep the drafted imagery and the descent of meaning, and fit one stress template:

| Line | Stress template | Appearance 1 (Act II.2) | Appearance 2 (Act III.1) | Appearance 3 (Act III.3) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 · 5 · 7 | *Èlena, sei mìa per sèmpre.* | *Èlena, mi pòrti dèntro,* | *Èlena, ogni còsa è spènta:* |
| 2 | 3 · 7 | *Io nel cuòre ti rimàngo.* | *come fèrro nel tuo pètto.* | *il bambìno, l'altro, il fuòco.* |
| 3 | 3 · 7 | *Nessun luògo ti nascònde* | *Fuggi pùre: in te dimòro,* | *Solo io rèsto nel silènzio,* |
| 4 | 3 · 7 | *dal tuo spòso, tuo signòre.* | *tuo padròne, tuo respìro.* | *tuo princìpio, tua rovìna.* |

Appearance 4 (the second offstage intrusion in the final aria) is line 1 of Appearance 1, unchanged.

A further seed, the composer's decision rather than the treatment's: at the wedding Vittorio's vow, "*Elena, ti prendo in sposa*", fits the same template as line 1 and is sung to the first two bars of the vision-phrase. Nobody can hear it as a warning at the wedding, because there is nothing yet to be warned of. It becomes legible only in retrospect, which is the opera's argument.
