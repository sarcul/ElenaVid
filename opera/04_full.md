# Elena: the full opera

### *Tragedia lirica in tre atti*, about two hours, built with the same code as the film

## Executive summary

The ten-minute film was a compressed telling. This is the whole opera: a prologue in the theatre, three acts with their preludes, an intermezzo, two intervals and the curtain calls, 40 sections and 2:00:24 in all. It is built with the same approach as the film, entirely in code, from the same libretto and the same musical material. No samples, footage, stock patches or generative-model output are used. The film's outputs are untouched: the full opera writes only to `output/full/` and to a cache outside the project folder.

The work was done in three stages, as for the film:

- **Score.** A composition engine sets every line of the libretto to music. It works from prosody to rhythm, then melody, harmony and orchestration.
  - Where the film had already set a line by hand, the engine reuses that tune: 103 of the 292 lines.
  - Four of the film's passages are quoted verbatim: the vow, the wedding chorus, the cooling and the blow.
- **Sound.** The same five tiers as the film (voices, orchestra, foley, environments and design), using the same instrument and voice models. It is rendered in 30 segments in parallel and mastered in one streaming pass.
- **Picture.** The same sets and shaders as the film. Where the film was cut by hand, the full opera is staged from the score's own data:
  - each section's location picks the set;
  - the singing picks who is framed;
  - the composed cues place the events.

  The opera house frames the evening, and the required shot recurs in the final aria.

## Flaws and limits

- **Most of the music is generated by rule, not composed by hand.** Only the film's 103 hand-set lines and its four quoted passages are hand-made. The other lines are set by the engine:
  - stresses are placed on strong beats by dynamic programming;
  - pitches come from a beam search over chord tones and stepwise motion;
  - phrases follow templates (open, close, contrast, climax).

  The result is correct and singable but formulaic in places. Phrase shapes recur, the harmonic rhythm is conventional, and the numbers were lengthened to reach two hours with repeats (da capo returns, cabalettas, codas).
- **Prosody is good but not perfect.** 89% of stressed syllables fall on strong beats (`elenavid qa prosody`). The rest are misplaced stresses an Italian listener would notice. The Latin numbers fit worst (the Requiem 62%, the Stabat Mater 74%), as does the duet "Domani, alla stessa ora" (65%).
- **The staging is rule-driven.** 273 shots are cut on phrase starts and framed on whoever sings.
  - The 2D sets were designed for one wide view each, so two-shots and singles enlarge the paper figures beyond what they were drawn for.
  - The figures hold poses (standing, seated, kneeling, arms rising, holding the child). They do not act or mouth the words.
- **One stage set for the act curtains.** The opera house has a single built set, the window and the sea of the final aria. The act curtains rise and fall on it, and the opening of each act dissolves into the story's own set before much of the stage is seen.
- **Two compromises of place.**
  - In N5, Vittorio proposes in the parlour as a recast third figure, because the parlour set has no man in its company.
  - In R5, "Marco's house" is staged in the Trieste street at dusk, as Marco walks away.
- **The voices are formant synthesis.** They carry pitch, words and colour, but no one will mistake them for singers.
  - The male voices came out quieter than Elena's. Measured against the orchestra in their own numbers, they sat 3 dB under it while she sat 4 dB over it, so each soloist has a fixed fader set from those measurements.
  - The whole mix was balanced by meters and level maps, not by ear.
- **Segmented rendering.** The audio is rendered in segments of about five minutes, each with its own reverb tails, so the joins are exact. A legato line cannot carry across a join, but the joins fall between numbers.
- **The intervals are 30 seconds.** A real theatre would take twenty minutes. Here they are a breath with applause, not a break.

## How it was made

### Score (`elenavid/full/`)

- **The libretto** is parsed from [`01_libretto.md`](01_libretto.md) into numbers, lines and stage directions.
- **The prosody module** syllabifies the Italian and Latin and marks the stresses. It handles synalepha, elision, dieresis and a small lexicon of exceptions.
- **The tune bank** matches each line against the film's hand-set lines. When the film set the same words, its tune is re-texted and moved into the singer's range.
- **New lines** are set by:
  - a rhythm search that puts stressed syllables on strong beats and the final stress on the last bar's downbeat;
  - a pitch search over the harmony, with cadence and contour targets.
- **Numbers** take the period's forms:
  - stanzas in lyric form, duets in thirds and sixths, cabalettas, codas and cadenzas;
  - recitatives over chords, and choruses harmonised in four parts.
- **The orchestra** has accompaniment styles from pad, arpeggio and *chitarra* to waltz, barcarolle, march and chorale.
- **The rules are checked at build time.** For example, the pitch class A never sounds after the Requiem, because Tommaso's tone was A.
- **Leitmotif statements** (Marco's three forms, Vittorio's phrase, the wedding melody and its lament) are tagged as the score is built. The picture draws them as lines of light.

### Sound (`elenavid/audio/mix_full.py`, `sounddesign_full.py`)

- **The cue sheet** gives every location its environment for its whole length, and places foley and design events on the composed cues:
  - doors, steps, paper and breath;
  - the train, the sea and the bora;
  - the pressure, sub hits and heartbeat of the design tier.
- **Rendering** runs in 30 segments of whole sections, six at a time. A segment is cached under a hash of everything that affects its sound, so a change re-renders only what it touches.
- **The mix** follows the film's mix:
  - calibration of the musical tiers;
  - dialogue priority for the soloists;
  - de-essing, a glue compressor and the true-peak limiter.

  It runs over the whole programme in 60-second chunks, each with a pre-roll so filter and envelope state carries across. The dramatic automation also carries over from the film: the dome bloom on Elena's voice in the final aria, and the muffled tunnel through which she hears Davide's grief.
- **Level rides** were made after reading the per-section level map. Applause in the intervals is down 5 dB, and the offstage fishermen's chorus in the sea interlude is down 6 dB.

### Picture (`elenavid/video/shots_full.py`, `render_full.py`)

- **Coverage.** Each sung scene is cut into shots of about 15 to 30 seconds, on the start of a sung line. A shot is a single when one voice dominates, a two-shot when two do, and the wide shot (or a vista of the set) when no one sings. The same framing never comes twice in a row.
- **Framing** is a frame centre and zoom applied over the composed picture (the `uCam` uniform). It is all zero in the film, so the film renders unchanged.
- **The opera house frames the evening.**
  - The orchestra is seen from the stalls, the pit, the boxes, the gallery and under the dome through the prologue, the preludes, the intermezzo and the intervals.
  - Each act ends with the curtain falling, and the house lights rise for the interval.
- **The required shot recurs in N. 22,** keyed to that number's cues:
  - an establishing crane down through the house during the orchestral introduction;
  - the slow dolly-in on the stage in golden light: she lifts her hands and sings, and the camera pushes past the conductor's shoulder;
  - the wide shot of the audience in their tiers;
  - her voice in the dome under the chandelier;
  - the tight close-up, neck taut and eyes closed, through the climax.
- **Text.** The surtitles, title cards and number captions are rendered when first needed and released when done.
- **Rendering** runs in 25 chunks of five minutes, as separate, resumable processes. The chunks are joined without re-encoding and the master audio is laid in.

## Reproducing it

The edition is chosen with `--edition full`; without it, every command builds the film.

```bash
elenavid score --edition full
```
```bash
elenavid audio --edition full --workers=6
```
```bash
elenavid video --edition full --jobs=2
```

The picture is encoded with NVIDIA's NVENC when the machine can run it and with x264 in software otherwise; `--encoder=x264` forces software. On a Windows laptop with two GPUs, OpenGL may run on the integrated one: setting the environment variable `SHIM_MCCOMPAT=0x800000001` for the render asks the NVIDIA driver to take it. It is a per-process variable, not a system setting.

## Disk use

- **`output/full/`: about 9.4 GB.**
  - The MP4 is 5.7 GB, the master WAV 2.1 GB and the stems 1.9 GB.
  - The MIDI, timeline, contact sheet and stills are small.
- **The render cache: about 21 GB.** It is `%LOCALAPPDATA%\elena_opera\cache\full` on Windows, `~/Library/Caches/elena_opera/cache/full` on macOS and `~/.cache/elena_opera/cache/full` on Linux, or wherever `ELENA_CACHE` points.
  - The audio segments take 16.0 GB and the picture chunks 5.0 GB.
  - Both can be deleted. The next run rebuilds whatever is missing: on this machine, about 20 minutes of audio rendering and mastering, and 30 minutes of picture.
- **The film's cache** (`output/audio/cache`, 1.7 GB) can also be deleted.
