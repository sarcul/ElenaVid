# Elena: the film

### A ten-minute motion-graphics opera, 16:9, made entirely in code

## Executive summary

*Elena* is condensed here into a continuous ten-minute film opera: every scene of the treatment, the twelve key numbers in digest, and the treatment's required shot, placed where it carries the most weight, at the final aria. Nothing in it is sampled, filmed or generated by an external model. Every note was composed as data, every sound is synthesised by models written for this project, and every frame is drawn by GLSL shaders. The soundtrack is mixed in five discrete tiers, delivered as stems.

| Deliverable | File |

## The frame: the film opens and closes in the same opera house

The film begins in the stalls of a teatro all'italiana before the curtain rises. The oboe gives the A, the orchestra tunes to it, the house applauds the conductor, the lights dim, and the title appears on the closed crimson curtain. The story then plays as a *teatro di luce*: layered, rim-lit, colour-coded silhouettes in volumetric haze, with the music drawn as light. At the final aria the walls of Elena's empty apartment fly out like stage flats, and we are back in the opera house for the required shot. It ends as the treatment asks: curtain on a single figure.

## Visual language

- **Colour as character.** Elena crimson; Marco verdigris green; Vittorio gold-edged at the wedding (no warning is visible, as the treatment insists), steel-blue as a vision; Davide amber; Tommaso a pale silver light; the chorus sepia.
- **Figures as light.** In the story scenes the characters are single soft silhouettes, rim-lit from the scene's own light source and casting shadows into it: an oval head, sloping shoulders, a waist, tapered arms with hands. Faces are left to the music and the text. Only the opera house frame gives Elena a modelled face, and only the close-up shows her in detail.
- **The music drawn as light.** The overlay layer draws the leitmotifs from the actual note data. The wedding tune is a gold ribbon trailing behind the music; in the *Stabat Mater* it is a tarnished bronze ribbon low in the frame. Vittorio's phrase is a steel line traced in the same place with the same shape on every appearance, so the eye sees that the melody never changes while the subtitles show the words descending (the vow draws it in gold). Tommaso's tone is a fixed horizontal line of silver at the height of A; at his death the frame keeps the place where it should be. Marco's motif is a green arc that rises and stops short, and completes only with the letter.
- **Typography.** Italian in Bodoni (the Parma face of the Verdi-era scores), English in Gill Sans beneath. Every syllable lights as it is sung, synchronised from the same note data as the audio. Visions and offstage lines sit at the top of frame, in cold silver.

## Sound design: five tiers

Each tier is rendered separately, with its own acoustic routing, and delivered as a stem. Nothing is a stock synthesiser patch: the orchestra and voices are physical and source-filter models, and the environments are procedural models of the actual sound sources.

| Tier | Contents | How it is made |
|---|---|---|
| **1 VOX** | Elena (soprano), Marco (tenor), Vittorio (baritone), Davide (bass-baritone), the Mother and Nives (mezzo), the railwayman, Tommaso (boy treble), SATB chorus of 24 | Formant singers: band-limited glottal-pulse wavetables cross-faded by vocal effort, jitter and shimmer, aspiration gated by the glottal cycle, six cascaded formants with soprano formant-tuning on high notes, and a singer's-formant boost for the men. An Italian and ecclesiastical-Latin phonemiser handles *c/g* softening, *gli*, *gn*, *sc*, *qu*, falling and rising diphthongs, stressed hiatus (*mi-a*, *Di-o*, *si-a*) and synalepha (sustain the first vowel). Consonants are sung before the beat so vowels land on it. Each chorus singer has their own detune, vibrato rate, formant scale and timing. |
| **2 SCORE** | The orchestra | Bowed strings as Helmholtz-motion sources with a bow-position comb, rosin noise at the slip, dynamic brightness, intonation drift, delayed vibrato and free bowing, through modal bodies (3 per section); 28 individually modelled string players. Double reeds and flute are source-filter with formants and breath; brass brightens with lip pressure and bends into the note. Karplus-Strong harp and pizzicato. Modal timpani, tam-tam (with its bloom), cymbal, triangle, tambourine. Tubular bells, and a church bell with true minor-third partials. Additive organ with chiff. |
| **3 FOLEY** | Footsteps on stone, boards and wood; silk; paper (the letter slides under the door, is opened and unfolded); doors (creak, close, slam); the blow (restrained: the picture cuts away); a body falling; breath (gasps, panic, sighs); glasses and cups; the suitcase; the cradle; the empty chair; the curtain falling | Procedural impulse and resonance models: heel-and-toe strikes through surface resonances, stick-slip creaks, sparse resonant crackle for paper and taffeta, modal glass and ceramic. |
| **4 ATMOS** | The hall's air and murmur, applause and coughs; the village market with swifts and cicadas; the campanile; parlour and Verona clocks; the bora with whistling rigging and rattling shutters; the night platform (steam, locomotive, pea whistle, steam whistle); the Adriatic, gulls, ship's horn and tram bell; church and room tones | The crowd walla is the voice engine babbling random Italian-like syllables; swifts are FM trills with Doppler pan sweeps; the sea is shaped noise with swell and wash; the locomotive is chuffs, hiss and rail joints accelerating. |
| **5 DESIGN** | Sub pressure under every vision; Elena's heartbeat; sub hits on the blow, the waking, the door; a reversed swell into the dolly-in; air rushing as the walls fly out; the ghost | Low-frequency and psychoacoustic design. **The ghost** is Vittorio's actual *"Èlena"* from the first vision, reversed, low-passed and dissolved in reverb. It appears only in Act III scene 3, inside Elena's perception. |

### Mix automation that carries the drama

- **Dialogue priority:** the orchestra yields up to 2.5 dB under the soloists.
- **The death:** nothing is added. The tone does not return, the bare fifth decays, and only the room and a distant sea remain (master level drops to about -50 dBFS).
- **Elena's perception (Act III.3, 7:32 to 7:38):** while Davide's grief breaks, the score tier remains warm and true. Score and voices are passed through a low-pass "tunnel", the atmosphere falls away, and her panicked breathing, a racing heartbeat and the ghost of Vittorio's voice enter from the design and foley tiers. The audience hears a grieving good man and understands that she hears a threat. This is the treatment's "subjective split", made possible by keeping the tiers separate.
- **The wide shot:** Elena's voice gains a dome-echo send; in the close-up her voice is drier and nearer.
- **Vittorio:** in the visions he is dry and close, inside her head; offstage he is distant, filtered and drenched in the hall.
- **Master:** 18 Hz high-pass, gentle glue compression (1.35:1), loudness to -16 LUFS integrated (ITU-R BS.1770 K-weighting with gating), true-peak limiting to -1 dBTP with 4x oversampling.

## How it was made, and its limits

- **Pipeline:** Python data, then numba DSP, then five stems and a master. Python shot logic drives GLSL 4.3 shaders through moderngl (HDR, bloom, ACES grade, grain), and frames are piped to the H.264 encoder (NVENC on an NVIDIA GPU, x264 otherwise). Everything is reproducible from the source:

```bash
elenavid score
```
```bash
elenavid audio
```
```bash
elenavid video
```

- **Honest limits.**
  - The singing is formant synthesis. It carries pitch, vowels, consonants, vibrato and phrasing, but it is not a human voice, and the Italian is approximate. The on-screen text carries intelligibility.
  - The orchestra is modelled, not sampled, so it is clearly not a recorded orchestra.
  - The figures are stylised; the close-up is a painted portrait, not photography.
  - I cannot listen to audio. The mix was balanced by measurement (per-tier level maps, spectrograms, loudness metering), not by ear.
